# Two design topics at once: the phone's bottom and the card back

Resolved. Product: Between Us.

**What happened.** Two conversations designed at the same time.
- The card back went four rounds. Round one drew a reference's ornaments; round four redrew a
  second reference's whole layout and pieces in the product's colours. The owner: "You just
  copied the card right? Why the fuck?" The stop after two rounds was in the Keeper and was not
  applied.
- The bottom of the phone screen showed two outline sketches. The owner had to ask "Do you think
  these designs are better?"; only then did the Keeper say they worked better but did not look
  better, and that it had asked the owner to judge looks from a drawing made for placement.
- In the card-back conversation the owner said what makes a design good for them: the balance of
  lines and black, small flourishes that grow out of the lines, areas cut out with their own
  design, lines that lead the eye, playfulness; and what fails: clean, predictable, safe, too even,
  empty space in random places. The bottom conversation, missing exactly that "something going
  on", never heard it.
**What the owner expected.** A partner that knows when its own work isn't good enough, stops in
time, uses references as lessons, and carries what was learned in one conversation to the others.
**Cause.**
- Rounds were not counted where anyone could see the count, so "two rounds" drifted.
- A round was shown without the Keeper's own verdict against today and against the owner's taste,
  so "tidier" passed for "better".
- A sketch was offered for judging on looks, which it can't show.
- What the owner said about taste stayed inside one conversation; nothing moved it to the files
  every conversation reads.
- A reference was taken as the thing to make. (Already covered by `design.md`, "References", and
  by the product's look notes.)
**What was changed.** In `design.md`: every round opens with its number and a third needs the
owner's say-so; a round comes with a plain verdict against today and against what the owner said
makes a design good, and "tidier" is not "better"; a sketch is judged on arrangement only. In
`SKILL.md`, "Keeping state.md": what the owner says that holds beyond the topic goes into the
product's files in the same turn. In Between Us, the owner's words on what makes a design good are
on its `state.md`.
